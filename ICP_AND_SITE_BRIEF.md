# Kindred — ICP & Website Brief

The site is built backward from this document. If a section of the site doesn't
serve someone named here, it shouldn't be on the page.

---

## Who we are building for

**One sentence:** People whose careers run on a few hundred relationships they
can't afford to let go cold — and who already run systems for everything else
in their lives.

They are not "people who want more friends." They already understand that
relationships are the highest-leverage asset they own. They are looking for a
**system**, because they've noticed they have one for revenue, one for fitness,
one for shipping code — and nothing at all for the people who got them here.

### The three profiles

**1. The Revenue Executive** — CRO, VP Sales, Head of BD, Regional Director
- 300–800 professional contacts; SaaS, tech, professional services, financial services
- Deals die from follow-up timing, not from bad product. Pipeline leaks at the relationship layer.
- Needs: follow-up that doesn't depend on memory, context before every call

**2. The Networking Executive** — CEO, Managing Director, Partner, Investor
- 500–1,500 contacts across industries; often the connector others rely on
- Meets 50+ people per conference; the overwhelming majority go cold within a month
- Loses track of who owes whom an introduction; reciprocity is invisible
- Needs: systematic follow-up after events, a record of favours in both directions

**3. The Growth Executive** — COO, VP Growth, Head of Partnerships, VP Marketing
- 200–500 contacts across functions; scale-up companies
- Multi-stakeholder deals; personal networking stops scaling as the company grows
- Needs: partnership pipeline that isn't a spreadsheet, stakeholder context

### What they have in common
- **200+ relationships that genuinely matter**, managed almost entirely by memory
- Heavy adopters of productivity tooling — they will happily learn a system
- Mobile-first; relationship work happens between meetings, in cars, at airports
- Allergic to sentimentality and to being sold at
- Treat their time as expensive, so the system must cost minutes, not hours

### What they are not
- Not sales teams looking for a team CRM (that's Salesforce/HubSpot's job)
- Not people who need convincing that relationships matter
- Not looking for a social network or another feed

## The core message

> **Kindred makes sure you never lose touch with the people who matter.**

Supporting thesis, and the argument the site is built on:

> You are deliberate about everything you value. Then you get to the people who
> built your career, and the entire system is "I'll remember." A system for your
> relationships isn't cold — it's how warmth survives a busy decade.

---

## Voice
- Conversational authority. Expert, no jargon, no hype.
- Specific over clever. Concrete scenarios beat abstract truths.
- Never sentimental, never transactional. The register is "one operator to another."
- No invented social proof. No fabricated statistics. No fake logos.

---

## Rules for examples on the site
- Example contacts must look like **this ICP's network**, not a personal address
  book: a board member, the person who made the introduction, a former colleague
  now at a target account.
- Always pair a name with **relationship context** — "who is this person to me"
  is the thing this user actually thinks in.
- Always label illustrative data as illustrative.

## What the site must contain (relaunch, not a teaser)

The previous site had real substance. The relaunch must have more, not less.

1. **Hero** — the thesis + the product visible immediately
2. **Who it's for** — the three profiles, named explicitly, so a visitor self-identifies
3. **The cost** — specific, recognisable scenarios from this ICP's life
4. **What we believe** — the mission/tribe section; systems are warmth, not coldness
5. **How the system works** — circles & cadence, gap tracking, one nudge
6. **The feature set in depth** — contacts & import, circles, interaction logging,
   smart reminders, timeline, notes, relationship strength, integrations
7. **Not a CRM** — the differentiation this audience asks for by name
8. **Privacy** — the most sensitive list they own; say what actually happens to it
9. **Pricing** — Free vs Plus ($19.99/mo, $199/yr)
10. **Writing** — the content engine
11. **FAQ** — the real objections: time cost, privacy, learning curve, CRM overlap
12. **CTA**

## Claims we may and may not make

**May** (verified in the product/codebase): contact details encrypted at rest;
circles with per-circle cadence; interaction logging across calls, messages,
email, meet-ups; smart reminders; timeline; notes with contact mentions;
relationship strength scoring; Gmail/Google Calendar integration; CalDAV
calendar sync; data export and deletion.

**May not** (unverified or invented — do not put on the site until confirmed):
- Any user counts, customer logos, or testimonials
- "3x more opportunities", "40% pipeline increase", or similar figures
- SOC 2 / GDPR certification claims — **confirm before publishing**
- "End-to-end encryption" as a blanket claim (contact fields are encrypted;
  that is a narrower and more accurate statement)
